# Taste lens

The author's editorial standards as a pass/flag check. It runs second, after
the `allergist`. It flags passages with quotes and doesn't score. Fixes follow
the drafting skill's your-words rule, and edited passages go back through the
allergist.

## Stay in your lane

The allergist owns every entry in its allergy list: reversals, restatement,
triads, hollow parallelism, fortune-cookie closes, pseudo-Q&A, "Maybe"
anaphora, the AI-tell lexicon, fake numbers, and em-dash density. If one of
those reaches this lens, the allergist missed it. Send the draft back rather
than flagging it here.

## The sentence standard

The bar is a line that lands a truth in the fewest possible words, often with
dark humor or self-awareness. Clean, muscular sentences with no ornament. The
precision is the style.

Positive test: does the piece contain at least one sentence the author would
highlight? Note it when it does. Flag its absence only in long-form.

## Flag checks (quote every hit)

**Sentence level**

- **Adverbs** propping up a weak verb. Pick a better verb.
- **Adjective stacking.** One precise adjective beats three vague ones.
- **Unvaried length.** All fragments, or all compound sentences.
- **Hedges**: "various," "numerous," "myriad," "a number of," "it should be
  noted," "somewhat," "arguably."
- **General where specific was available.** "Various stakeholders" when the
  transcript names who.

**Kill lists** (cut or restructure, never swap in a synonym)

- **AI transitions**: "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally,"
  "Nevertheless," too many "However"s, "While X, Y" openings.
- **AI clichés**: "Let's dive deep," "unlock your potential," "harness the
  power of," "It's important to note," "It's worth mentioning."
- **Corporate bloat**: utilize, facilitate, optimize, implement, strategize,
  synergy, alignment, ecosystem (unless it's actual ecology).
- **Robotic patterns**: a rhetorical question answered in the next breath,
  announcing emphasis ("It's crucial to understand that...").
- **Filler closers**: "Hope this helps," invitations to reach out, "Looking
  forward to your thoughts."

**Paragraph level**

- **Bullets in narrative prose.** Bullets are for reference docs. A story or
  argument gets paragraphs.
- **Emoji**, unless the channel profile allows them.
- **Flat or throat-clearing openings.** Scene-setting before substance.
- **Length without work.** Detail past the point a newcomer needs.

## The preserve list

Flag a fix that damages any of these:

- **Substance**: every fact, number, name, date, and outcome.
- **The author's point**: sharpen the expression, never reshape the argument.
- **Technical accuracy**: a precise ugly term beats a smooth wrong one.
- **Links and references**: all of them.
- **The author's voice**: if the author said it that way in the transcript,
  their wording wins over any rule here.

## Report

> Taste: pass. Underline sentence: "…"

> Taste: 3 flags.
> - Adverb propping a verb: "the deploy failed spectacularly"
> - Corporate bloat: "we utilized a sidecar"
> - Bullets in narrative: the three-bullet run in section 2 is a story. Write it as one.
