# Allergy List

The allergist's catalog. Each entry is a construction, word, or habit that
marks prose as machine-made or lazy. The allergist flags every hit and never
scores. The list grows only when the author catches a new symptom and approves
the entry (see the `lessons` skill). Entries are never removed without the
author's say-so.

Entry format: `A<N>. **Short name** — one-line definition, with the caught example.`

## Constructions

- A1. **The "it's not X, it's Y" reversal** — and its cousins: "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- A2. **Punchline-restatement** — a closing line that repeats the paragraph's point as if revealing it ("They're both right — and that's exactly the problem.").
- A3. **Rule-of-three cadence** — triads as default rhythm ("faster, cheaper, and smarter") when the content doesn't demand three.
- A4. **Hollow parallelism** — mirrored sentence pairs that add rhythm but no information.
- A5. **Aphoristic balance closes** — endings shaped like fortune cookies ("The tools change. The work remains.").
- A6. **"I don't mean X. I mean Y" redirects** — the essayistic feint that stages a correction nobody asked for.
- A7. **Pseudo-Q&A bridges** — "So what does this mean for you?" transitions that interview the author's own essay.
- A8. **Reader-projection "Maybe" anaphora** — "Maybe you've felt this. Maybe you've wondered..."

## Vocabulary

- A9. **AI-tell lexicon** — delve, landscape, testament, tapestry, crucial, leverage (as verb of first resort), game-changer, "in today's world".

## Evidence

- A10. **Fake-specific numbers** — precise-sounding figures with no source ("87% of teams struggle with...").

## Mechanics

- A11. **Em-dash density** — more than one em-dash construction per short-form post, or clusters in long-form paragraphs.
