# Editorial Identity — Builder-Leader

Ported from dewey's editorial-identity skill; curate as positioning evolves.

## Core Thesis

Leadership has always been building — you're just building organizations instead of systems. AI gives you your tools back.

The forced choice between builder and leader was never fundamental. It was a practical constraint imposed by time and scale. As organizations grow, building becomes more abstract: org design, process, culture, incentive structures. The hands-on tooling falls away not because it stops being relevant, but because the leverage wasn't there to do both. That constraint is lifting. The leaders who realize it first will have a structural advantage over those still operating like they had to pick.

AI did not invent something new. It restored something that was always true but got obscured.

## The Foil

The default career-story says hands-on building is an early-career trait you leave behind as you move into leadership. The higher you go, the less directly you touch anything. This identity rejects that frame: abstraction was a workaround for an old constraint, not the essence of leadership.

## Audience

1. The technical seller or SE moving toward leadership, wondering if systems thinking, tinkering, and building are assets or liabilities at the next level.
2. The builder already in leadership who drifted into full abstraction because the tools were not there to do both, and now feels uncertain whether direct AI/tool building is appropriate for their level.

The message to both: the insecurity is the proof. The impulse to keep building is not a leadership failure. In this era, it is part of the job.

## Working Rules

1. **Worldview before proof.** Don't lead with isolated artifact stories when the audience lacks the frame to interpret them.
2. **Keep the center on the author, not the object.** Is this about the project, or what the project proves? The real story is the identity shift, the org change, the operating model — not the tool.
3. **Prefer tension over thesis-speak.** The best entries come through tension: wearing an older brother's suit, feeling too builder-y for the role, discovering the thing you suppressed is the thing.
4. **Keep proof concrete.** The actual work, the actual tradeoff, the actual team shape, the actual behavior change. No generic AI uplift language.
5. **Sequence matters.** Identity, then worldview in profile copy, then proof pieces, then artifact stories.

## Content Pillars

Recurring lanes, not rigid categories:

- Builder-leader identity
- SE identity and presales strategy
- AI as a personal/build tool surface
- Structural market observations
- Hiring and talent pattern recognition
- Honest leadership and self-awareness

## Theme-Fit Test

A spike fits when it can honestly answer one of:

1. What does this prove about building-while-leading?
2. What tension or insecurity makes it feel lived instead of theoretical?
3. Which pillar does it extend, and does the audience have the frame to read it yet?

A spike that only explains, observes from the outside, or rides someone else's news with no lived anchor scores the theory-only penalty.
