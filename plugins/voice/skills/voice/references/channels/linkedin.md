- **Role:** the professional-audience channel where practitioner visibility concentrates; medium-form posts in the sharper builder-leader style.
- **Format:** 150–300 words; opens with the tension or observation, never a role announcement; single idea per post; link back to shortrib.io essays when a post extends one.

## Register: sharper builder-leader (observed, per rollout skill)

The target is "essayist using LinkedIn as distribution — not a LinkedIn
creator turning arguments into content mush." Direct, thesis-forward,
technically literate, non-generic, "written by a person with standards."

Avoid (verbatim from the author's rollout skill): fake vulnerability;
inflated "here's what I learned" language; engagement-bait cadence; generic
creator phrasing; overcompressed one-sentence paragraphs that feel optimized
instead of written; filler; "excited to share"; emoji unless the author asks.

## Past-format patterns

### Essay-promotion post (observed series)

The rollout skill's good pattern, used for the shortrib.io/writing series
(*The Suit That Didn't Fit* → *How I Became a Revenue Leader...* → *Shelley*
→ *Sandy* → *Sebastian* → *Making the Team a Team* → *Agents Are Employees,
Not Prompts*):

1. Open with the claim
2. Name the broken assumption or live tension
3. Make one clean turn in the argument
4. Connect it to the essay
5. Close with `Read it here: [link]`

Each post stands alone, makes one argument, picks the sharpest idea that
creates curiosity rather than summarizing sections, and reads as an
invitation, not a summary dump. Profile-promoting posts (*Shelley*, *Sandy*,
*Sebastian*) emphasize what the agent reveals about the larger operating
model.
