## Past-format patterns (observed)

Three recurring shapes in the published corpus:

### Worldview essay (~1,100–1,400 words)

*The Suit That Didn't Fit* (https://shortrib.io/writing/the-suit-that-didnt-fit/)
is the archetype: lived moment / controlling metaphor → the received template
named → the reframe (constraint, not truth) → present-tense proof from his
own work → the audience addressed directly ("There are two groups of people
who need to hear that") → imperative close in second person.

### Argument essay (~900–1,300 words)

*You Can't Build Better Software Without Reordering Status*
(https://shortrib.io/writing/you-cant-build-better-software-without-reordering-status/):
irritant or confession → why the easy reading is wrong → self-implication →
historical rhyme (offshoring, agile, SOA) → stakes → challenge close ("We can
bury it. The question is whether we'll have the humility to do it.").

### Agent profile (~600–800 words)

*Sandy* (https://shortrib.io/writing/sandy/): who was hired and why in one
breath → the organizational problem generalized → what the work actually was
→ why it's more than it looks → what changed for the humans → closing
`## On the name` section. Profiles emphasize what the agent reveals about the
larger operating model, not character fiction (linkedin-writing-rollout
skill).

## Conventions (observed)

- Essay bodies are pure paragraphs: no headers, bullets, or bold (profiles' "On the name" is the lone header in the corpus).
- Titles are Title Case declarative claims; profiles titled with the agent's name alone.
- Lowercase hyphenated tags at the foot; reuse existing tags.
- One self-link per piece max, woven into a sentence; generous inline links to primary sources.
