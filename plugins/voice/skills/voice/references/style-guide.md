# Style Guide

> Draft derived from published corpus — pending author approval pass.
> House rules for the text itself; the voice guide is who's talking. Every
> rule below is either observed in the seven pieces read at
> shortrib.io/writing (Feb–Jun 2026) or carried forward from the seed
> (marked *seed*). Where a rule is inferred rather than directly observed,
> it says so.

## Titles

- Title Case, observed consistently: *You Can't Build Better Software Without Reordering Status*, *Builder-Leaders Finally Get Their Tools Back*.
- Declarative claims, usually full sentences. The title states the thesis; the essay earns it.
- Not observed anywhere in the corpus, so don't produce them: colon constructions ("X: Why Y Matters"), questions, numbered listicles, gerund-titled explainers ("Understanding Agent Governance").
- Profile pieces take the agent's name alone as the title: *Sandy*, *Shelley*, *Sebastian*.
- No clickbait constructions; the hook is a real observation, preferably in the author's own transcript words (drafting mines hooks from the transcript first). (*seed*)

## Length norms (observed)

- Essays: roughly 900–1,400 words (*Governance* ~900; *The Suit That Didn't Fit* ~1,400).
- Agent profiles: roughly 600–800 words (*Sandy* ~700).
- Short-form says one thing; long-form earns its length through the story, not through coverage. (*seed*)
- LinkedIn posts: concise enough to read in-feed; one argument; see channel profile.

## Formatting

- Essays are pure paragraphs: the corpus contains **no headers, no bullet lists, no bold, no code blocks** in essay bodies. Structure lives in the argument, not the markup.
- One exception observed: profiles close with a single `## On the name` section (*Sandy*).
- Paragraphs run 2–6 sentences. Single-sentence paragraphs are verdicts, used once or twice per piece ("Today it doesn't.").
- Minimal emphasis; no emoji anywhere in the corpus. (*seed*, confirmed)
- Em-dashes sparing — allergy A11 caps density at one construction per short-form post.
- Blockquotes only for genuinely quoted people (Dan Shipper, Claire Vo quotes run inline in the corpus; when quoting at length, attribute by name and role).

## Punctuation and diction habits (observed)

- Contractions by default; deliberate un-contraction for emphasis ("I do not think they are conscious" — *Agents Are Employees, Not Prompts*).
- Conversational parentheticals are house style: "(ok, our)", "(were you?)".
- Coinages take quotes on first use: "I've taken to using 'builder-leader' to describe us" (*Builder-Leaders Finally Get Their Tools Back*); unquoted thereafter.
- Mild profanity permitted when it carries weight; observed at most once per essay ("why the fuck is it doing that" — *The Biggest Risk...*). Never in titles. LinkedIn/professional posts: none unless the author supplies it.
- Serial commas in use.

## Links

- Inline, on the meaningful phrase, generous to primary sources — Mollick's *Management as AI Superpower*, Harper Reed's workflow post, *The New Kingmakers* all linked in-text. (*seed rule confirmed*)
- People are credited by name and role when quoted or borrowed from: "as writer/builder/leader/podcaster Dan Shipper put it".
- Self-linking: at most one link back to his own prior essay per piece, woven into the sentence (*Builder-Leaders* links "a suit that never quite fit" back to *The Suit That Didn't Fit*). Never link-bait own content more than once per piece. (*seed*)
- LinkedIn posts point back to the source essay with `Read it here: [link]` (linkedin-writing-rollout skill).

## Numbers

- Only real ones, and only ones the author owns: "save more than 50% of my costs", "I built my first team in 2004", "more than twenty years". If a number isn't verifiable, it doesn't appear (allergy A10: fake-specific numbers). (*seed*, confirmed — the corpus contains no unowned statistics)
- Approximations stated as approximations ("more than 50%", not "52.3%").

## Tags (blog)

- Lowercase, hyphenated topic tags at the foot of each piece: `leadership`, `ai`, `agents`, `builders`, `org-design`, `context-engineering`, etc. Reuse existing tags before minting new ones.
